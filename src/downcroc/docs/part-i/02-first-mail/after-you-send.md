# After you send

Once a batch is on its way, the question changes from "what will happen?" to
"what did happen?" downcroc keeps that answer for you, message by message, so
you can come back minutes or days later and read the outcome instead of
relying on memory.

## Looking at the batch

From the **batch** menu, choose **show** and name the batch. What you get is
the batch as a whole: its name, how many messages it holds, and where each one
stands. A [[job]] that has gone out will show as [[sent]]; one that is waiting
will show as [[queued]]; one that ran into trouble will tell you so.

Reading the batch view is often enough. It answers "did this send finish?" and
"did anything get stuck?" in one place, without making you chase individual
messages.

## Looking at the messages themselves

When you want to see the messages rather than the container, go to **job** and
choose **list**. The list shows jobs across your work, and you can narrow it
to the batch you care about. This is the view to use when one message in a
large send needs your attention and you want to find it by name or by state.

Between the batch view and the job list, you can always trace a send from the
decision to the result: which [[batch]] it belonged to, which job carried it,
and what became of it. If a conversation continued in replies, the stored mail
keeps that thread together so the story of a message does not end at "sent".
